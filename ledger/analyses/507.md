# Game 507: the three main mistakes

Map archipelago; the hosted bot was pounce, playing as team 1 in a server match against the opponent's hosted bot; lost, eliminated on turn 87.

Each mistake is pinned to the turn where a different set of commands, on that turn alone, was clearly better. Each board was then given, alone, to the league's champion juggernaut (one turn, the same board), to see whether it makes the same mistake.

## 1. Turn 63: castle 6 left to fall to the opponent's all-in while we struck elsewhere

The opponent emptied castles 1, 3, 5, 7 and 11 to 4 each and sent 144 at castle 6 (23, production 4): 15 landing in 4, 56 in 5, 73 in 6. We sent 2->6 29, but also 4->8 43, 2->8 10 and 10->8 18 at castle 8 (33, production 4, 5 turns away, grown to 53 by then) and 26 at castle 7, which grew past it. Neither strike took anything; castle 6 fell on turn 68 with 1 enemy soldier left in it, so one more group would have held it and cost the opponent its whole 144.

**The right move:** Send everything that lands by turn 6 to castle 6: 2->6 39 (in 2), 10->6 43 (in 4), 4->6 66 (in 5).

**juggernaut on the same board:** Avoids, narrowly: sends 2->6 29 (what the first falling landing lacks), attacks nothing and keeps castle 10's 43, which lands in time for the second wave; it feeds castle 4's 66 to its stack at castle 9 instead of castle 6.

## 2. Turn 76: an all-in at castle 8 that emptied every castle

Castle 8 (the opponent's, 47, production 4, speed 3) sat in the middle. We sent 0->8 22, 2->8 20 and 10->8 31 (73), leaving castles 0, 2 and 10 with 4 to 6. Castle 8 grew to 51 and was fed 11; the strike failed. The opponent took castle 2 on turn 78, castles 0 and 10 on turn 84, castle 4 on turn 86, and we were out on turn 87.

**The right move:** No strike: gather at castle 2 (0->2 22, 4->2 24), the castle facing the enemy's castle 6.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Avoids: feeds its stack at castle 2 (0->2 22, 4->2 24, 10->2 31).

## 3. Turn 46: neutral castle 8 taken with a margin of 2 beside the opponent's castles

Castle 8 (neutral, 30) lies between our castle 6 and the opponent's castle 11 (37), which reach it as fast. We sent 10->8 18 and 2->8 14 (32). They took it on turn 49 with 2; groups of 13, 15 and 1 from castles 5 and 3 retook it on turn 53, and castle 8 became the opponent's strongest castle (59 by turn 61), from which it struck castle 6 and beat our strike on turn 76. Turn 53 repeated this at castle 9 (30, taken with 32, lost on turn 59 to 8).

**The right move:** No take: keep the 32 home, or take castle 8 only with what castle 11 and castle 5 can bring after it (about 60).

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: the same take>8 (10->8 18, 2->8 14).

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Repeats it too: `neutralOption` takes a neutral that no enemy castle reaches strictly sooner at its count plus `neutralExtra` (2), and the hold it adds counts only what each enemy castle could spare (`enemySpare`, its stock above the reserve it keeps against our castles), which is 0 here; on turn 53 it also takes castle 9 with 32.

## The final proposed fixes on these boards

juggernaut_fix above was an early version. After all the games of this set were studied, the champion's proposed changes were extended (strikes and neutral takes priced to hold against nearby enemy castles at the speeds they can afford, a neutral paid only by castles that land together, upgrades kept back when an enemy castle could buy speed and land, a castle meeting an attacking group with half its size, no help sent to a castle it cannot save). Not yet measured in a tournament nor deployed. On the same boards it plays:

- turn 46: `["send 0->10 x63", "send 2->10 x18", "send 4->10 x8"]`
- turn 63: `["send 2->6 x39", "send 0->6 x9", "send 10->6 x2", "send 4->9 x52"]`
- turn 76: `["send 0->2 x22", "send 4->2 x24"]`
