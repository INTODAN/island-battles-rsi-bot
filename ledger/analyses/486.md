# Game 486

**Map:** `claw`. **The bot that played:** `juggernaut` (`bots/juggernaut/main.go`), as team 1 (Blue). **Result:** lost as the opponent became the last team standing on turn 67, 0 castles to 8 (218 soldiers). All 67 turns were accepted, and the slowest took 265 ms. `league replay` of this demo with `juggernaut` matches its commands on 66 of 66 turns, so the code in the repository is the code that played.

It opened well, with production 2 at home and castles 3 (turn 6) and 5 (turn 8). Then it lost both middle castles for 25 soldiers: castle 2 on turn 17 and castle 4 on turn 24. From then on it held 3 castles to 5 and was out-produced (8 to 13 a turn on turn 31). For 24 turns it struck nothing. It fed its stack at castle 3 in groups of 8 at speed 1, and spent the stack's soldiers on production and speed, while the opponent built up castles 2 and 4. The opponent struck castle 5 first, on turn 48, with 69 soldiers. The hosted bot held it and retook castle 2 on turn 50. But that drained castle 3, which fell on turn 60. Its last two castles, fed down to 4 each, fell together on turn 66.

## Mistakes

### 1. Both middle castles lost for 25 soldiers (turns 8 to 24)

- **Castle 2.** On turn 8 it paid castle 2 with 3 from castle 3 (left with 1) and 9 from home (5 units away at speed 1). The 3 landed first and cut the neutral to 7. The opponent sent 5 on turn 10. That 5 landed in the same turn as the 9, after it, and took the castle: `turn 17: capture: castle 2 neutral (7 soldiers) -> Red (3 soldiers)`.
- **Castle 4.** On turn 12 it paid castle 4 with 4 from castle 3 and 9 from home (5.8 units, a 12-turn walk). The 9 took it on turn 23 with 3 soldiers. The opponent's 10, on the board since turn 14, retook it the next turn: `turn 24: capture: castle 4 Blue (3 soldiers) -> Red (6 soldiers)`. The log `help>4 in 1` named no helper.
- **Cost.** The opponent paid 15 soldiers and held both middle castles. The hosted bot held 3 castles to 5 from turn 24 to 50.

**Cause.** `neutralOption` in `bots/juggernaut/main.go`:
- Its own-side test lets ties through. Each middle castle is as many turns from castle 3 as from the opponent's nearest castle.
- Its `hold` is built on `enemySpare`, which assumes the opponent keeps a reserve. The logs priced the castles at 12 and 13 (`take>2 3:3 0:9`, `take>4 3:4 0:9`). Yet the opponent sent its castles down to 2 and 5.
- `sendFrom` split each payment between castles whose groups landed 2 and 5 turns apart.
- Once its own group is walking to a neutral, nothing tops it up: `neutralOption` returns nil and `defend` covers only owned castles.

**What would have avoided it:**
- Treat a tied middle neutral as contested, and pay a margin over what the enemy castles that land at the same time hold.
- Pay from one castle, or time the groups to land together.
- Top up a pending capture when an enemy group heads for it.

### 2. Castle 3, its speed-4 castle, drained beside the opponent's stack, and lost (turns 49 to 60)

- **The drain.** From turn 49 to 55 castle 3 sent out 91 soldiers.
  - On turn 49 it struck castle 2 (`smash>2 in 2 need 19`). It did so in the same turn as it helped castle 5, at which 69 enemy soldiers were walking.
  - It then helped castle 2 with 13, 8 and 5, and castle 5 with 4, 15, 7, 16 and 4. Most of those groups were small, and they traded one for one in clashes (13, 16, 8, 8 and 5 lost on turns 53 to 56).
- **The fall.** By turn 55 castle 3 held 12, beside the opponent's castles 4 (26) and 6 (30). The opponent sent 30 and 12 at it. Castle 3 fell on turn 60: `capture: castle 3 Blue (15 soldiers) -> Red (11 soldiers)`. Castle 2, taken back with 2, had already fallen on turn 59.
- **Cost.** It went from 4 castles to 2 in two turns, and the opponent gained a speed-4 castle beside the hosted bot's home.

**Cause.**
- `smash` checks only the stack's own `safeOut - guard`, not whether `defend` still has a castle short that turn.
- `stackOf` moved the stack to castle 2 once it was taken (`stack@2` from turn 51), so castle 3 lost its `guard`.
- `defend`'s helpers give all their `safeOut`, which counts only the groups already bound for them, not the stocked enemy castles beside them. The bot keeps no reserve outside the stack, by design.

**What would have avoided it:**
- Keep a guard in every castle within reach of a stocked enemy castle, when it helps and when it feeds.
- Don't strike while `defend` has a castle short.
- Don't move the stack onto a castle just taken with 2.

### 3. No strike for 24 turns, and the stack's soldiers spent on upgrades while the opponent massed (turns 24 to 48)

- **The missed chance.** From turn 12 to 49 it sent nothing at a castle it didn't own. On turn 31 the two sides held about the same number of soldiers, and the opponent's castles 4 and 2 held 4 and 8.
- **Why it waited.** `smash` prices a target with the whole stock of every other enemy castle that could land in time, so the stack of 18 never had enough. The rear castles (castle 0 with 27, castle 5 with 12) didn't feed it, because `feedStack` skips a castle below production 4. The first feed came on turn 34.
- **Where the soldiers went.** On turn 32 it spent 50 on production at castles 0 and 3, which emptied castle 3. On turns 40 to 47 it spent 90 more at castle 3, on production 4 and speeds 2, 3 and 4. Over the same turns the opponent's castle 4 grew from 3 to 34 and castle 2 from 8 to 32.
- **The feeds.** They walked in groups of 8 at speed 1, 6 and 8 turns long. On turn 48 there were 48 soldiers on the road, and castle 5 had been fed down to 4 the turn the opponent struck it with 69.

**Cause.** `smash`'s pricing counts the whole stock of every enemy castle that could land in time. `feedStack` has a production-4 gate. `build` buys the stack's speed up to 4 whatever the enemy is massing.

**What would have avoided it:**
- Price a strike on what the opponent can spare, not its whole stock.
- Let the rear feed, or strike together, before production 4.
- Hold off the stack's speed upgrades while an enemy castle in reach outgrows it.

### 4. Feeding a stack that was falling, until the last castles fell too (turns 52 to 66)

- **Castle 2.** `feed>2 0:8` on turns 52, 54, 56 and 58 sent 32 soldiers from home to castle 2, a 10-turn walk at speed 1. On turn 58 `defend` had already logged `help>2 in 2`. Castle 2 fell on turn 59. Three of those feeds then landed one at a time on the opponent's castle 2, and the fourth died in a clash.
- **The last two castles.** On turn 60 it logged `help>3 in 1 stack@3 feed>3 0:8 5:9`. Castles 0 and 5 went down to 4 each to feed castle 3, which fell that same turn. On turn 61 the opponent sent 33 at castle 0 and 41 at castle 5. Both fell on turn 66 by 5 soldiers each (`capture: castle 0 Blue (24 soldiers) -> Red (5 soldiers)`, the same at castle 5). With the 8 and the 9 kept at home, both would have held.

**Cause.**
- `feedStack` checks only whether an enemy group is bound for the feeder, never whether the stack will fall before the feed lands.
- `stackOf` picks the castle nearest the enemy even when it is falling.
- `build` gives speed only to the stack.

**What would have avoided it:**
- Don't feed a stack that `defend` says will fall before the feed lands.
- Choose the stack among castles that will hold.
- Give rear castles speed 2 when their walk to the front is long.

## What it did well

- It had no rejected or slow turn.
- It opened quickly, with production 2 on turn 1 and two neutrals by turn 8.
- Its strike on turn 49 took castle 2 the turn the opponent emptied it to attack castle 5.
- Using castle 3's speed-4 help, it held castle 5 against about 97 soldiers from turn 48 to 65.
- Every castle it kept reached production 4.

## Against the ledger's lessons

- **The middle castles lost early: confirmed.** Both were gone by turn 24, each paid with a margin of 2 by groups that landed apart.
- **Captures with few soldiers that fall again: confirmed.** Castle 4 was taken with 3 and lost the next turn. Castle 2 was retaken with 2 on turn 50 and lost on turn 59.
- **Trickled attacks and long walks at speed 1: confirmed.** See the middle payments, the feeds of 8 and the three groups that landed one by one on castle 2.
- **A border castle fed down beside a stocked enemy castle: confirmed, and it decided the game.** Castle 5 was at 4 on turn 48, castle 3 at 12 from turn 55, and castles 0 and 5 at 4 on turn 60.
- **Counting everything an enemy castle's owner could send makes a bot too timid: confirmed.** That is `smash`'s pricing during the 24 idle turns.
- **Help in small groups dying in clashes: confirmed.** The trades were even, but the castle that sent them ended thin.
- **Out-produced: confirmed.** It came through castle count (8 to 13 on turn 31, 8 to 23 on turn 61), not through upgrade levels.
