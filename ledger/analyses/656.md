# Game 656

**Map:** `claw`. **The bot that played:** `juggernaut`, as team 1 (Blue). This was a server match imported later: the game's record says the bot ran as a localhost bot in the 3D client, and `turns.jsonl` is empty. **Result:** lost at the generation limit (299 turns played) on castle count, 2 castles to 6, with 1,640 soldiers to 1,917.

**The bot that played is not `bots/juggernaut/main.go`.** Its logs carry tokens that code never writes (`meet<N>` and `lost` in its help notes, e.g. on turns 250 and 254), and no branch has them. `league replay` of this demo with `juggernaut` matches only 28 of 299 turns. On 183 turns the code would have struck and the bot did not, for example castle 4 for 120 on turn 64. Its logged strike prices do follow `smash`'s formula: on turn 246, `need 611` = 601 + 4×2 + 2. So the difference lies in what it keeps back from a strike. The causes below are read from its logs, which are complete. The functions named are those of `main.go` whose logic the logs match.

The two sides split the middle: the opponent took castle 4 on turn 40, and the hosted bot took castle 2 on turn 42. Each then had 4 castles and 15 or 16 production. The game froze for 200 turns. No castle changed hands from turn 42 to 247. The hosted bot piled everything on castle 2 (3,095 by turn 244), while the opponent kept a similar total spread over four castles. On turn 244 the opponent moved 776 out of castle 1. The hosted bot then took castle 4 on turn 247 with an exact strike of 611. But it fed its whole stack forward, left its other castles at 4 to 12, and lost castles 5 (turn 253), 2 (257), 5 again (266), 0 (268) and 7 (271). It spent the last 28 turns with about 1,500 soldiers in two castles and never struck again.

## Mistakes

### 1. The stack fed forward and left the middle castle bare (turns 248 to 257)

- **Turn 248.** After the capture of castle 4 the stack moved there. Castle 2 sent everything: `help>4 in 2 2:767 2:174 stack@4 feed>4 2:1581 3:8`, 2,522 soldiers, leaving 12. The 941 of help already covered the opponent's 776 bound for castle 4. The extra 1,581 stripped castle 2, two turns from the opponent's castles 6 (781) and 7 (828).
- **Turn 249.** The opponent sent groups of 46 to 86 at all four of the hosted bot's thin castles. It took castle 5 on turn 253.
- **Turns 250 to 252.** The hosted bot rebuilt castle 2 with 403 on turn 250, then fed it down to 4 again on turn 252 (`feed>4 2:305`).
- **Turn 257.** Castle 2 fell to 27 soldiers sent in two groups: `capture: castle 2 Blue (12 soldiers) -> Red (3 soldiers)`. At that moment 878 sat in castle 0 and 804 in castle 4, two turns away at speed 4.

**Cause.** `feedStack` sends a castle's whole `safeOut` whenever no enemy group is bound for it, whatever stocked enemy castles stand beside it. `stackOf` picks a new stack after each capture, which turns the old stack into a rear castle that the next feed empties.

**What would have avoided it:**
- A castle within `guardWalk` of a stocked enemy castle keeps its guard when it feeds.
- A stack that moves leaves behind what its old castle needs to hold.

### 2. Two hundred turns without a strike, ahead in soldiers (turns 64 to 244)

- **The log.** Every turn read `stack@2 feed>2 ...`, with no `smash`. Home grew from 265 on turn 64 to 3,095 on turn 244.
- **The target.** The opponent's castle 4 held 109 on turn 64, 217 on turn 100 and 517 on turn 200. It sat 3 units from the stack, at production 3 and speed 1.
- **The lead.** The hosted bot had the most soldiers from turn 114 to 285.
- **How it ended.** Only when the opponent emptied castle 1 on turn 244 did a strike come (`smash>4 in 2 need 611`, turn 246).

**Cause.** The decisions fit a stack guard that counts the whole stock of every enemy castle. On turn 64 that leaves 257 − 252 = 5. On turn 100 the guard of 792 is over the stack's 783. This is an inference from the logs; `main.go`'s `guard()` counts only castles within 4 turns.

Against an opponent whose total stock is spread evenly and matches the stack, such a guard never lets a strike go.

**What would have avoided it:** guard against what the enemy can spare and land before help arrives, not its whole hoard. Castles that can't all come at once don't all need to be kept against.

### 3. The last 28 turns idle while losing on castles (turns 272 to 299)

- **The log.** It was `stack@3` and nothing more, apart from one feed and a 40-soldier speed purchase on turn 298.
- **The board on turn 280.**
  - The hosted bot held castle 3 with 862 and castle 4 with 668 at speed 4.
  - The opponent held castles 0 (59), 1 (40), 5 (59) and 7 (36), each three turns or less from one of them, besides castle 2 (675) and castle 6 (568).
- **What it cost.** Two captures would have drawn the game, and three would have won it.

**Cause.** `smash` strikes only from the stack, with `spare = safeOut − guard`. The guard of castle 3 (castles 2, 6, 0 and 5) was well over its stock. `finishing()` lengthens the allowed walk near the end, but never lowers the guard when the castle count is already lost.

**What would have avoided it:**
- When the castle count loses near the end, strike from every castle and keep only what holds the count.
- Let a castle other than the stack strike.

### 4. Strikes from the wrong castle, over long walks, then trickled (turns 245 to 268)

- **Turn 258.** `smash>2 in 5 need 112` struck castle 2 from castle 0 at speed 2, though castle 4 (780, speed 4) was 2 turns from it. The opponent saw it coming, sent 92, and the 112 left castle 2 with 3 for the opponent.
- **Turns 259 to 268.** `smash>1 in 10 need 148` walked 10 turns across the map. The opponent sent 87 in time. That strike was followed by `need 5` (turn 266) and `need 14` (turn 268), and the 5 died in a clash.
- **Cost.** 279 soldiers for nothing. The `smash>1 in 3 need 18` of turn 245 had failed the same way, after the opponent bought speed at castle 6.

**Cause.** `smash` strikes only from `stackOf`'s castle. It counts enemy help only from castles whose help lands before the strike, so a long walk invites help that it doesn't price.

**What would have avoided it:**
- Strike from the nearest fast castle that can pay.
- Prefer walks shorter than the enemy's reaction, or count a long walk's help as certain.

### 5. Home emptied by a stack switch (turns 260 to 268)

- **Turn 260.** `stack@4 feed>4 0:601` left home castle 0 with 4, three turns from the opponent's castle 2 (107, then 660 from turn 265).
- **Turn 266.** The opponent sent 27 at castle 0, and the logs read `help>0 in 2 lost`.
- **Turn 268.** `capture: castle 0 Blue (8 soldiers) -> Red (15 soldiers)`.
- **The ping-pong.** Then the stack went back and forth between castles 3 and 4 with 800 soldiers (`feed>3 4:796` on turn 269, `feed>4 3:800` on 271, `feed>3 4:800` on 275), each move a walk of 3.2 units.

**Cause.** `stackOf` and `feedStack`, as in mistake 1.

### 6. Captures that fell within a few turns, and help that died on the road (turns 251 to 271)

- **Castle 5.** It was retaken on turn 259 and lost again with 0 on turn 266. Its help (`speed3@0 0:14`, turn 262) bought speed for 20 to send 14, and the 14 died in a clash.
- **Castle 7.** It was taken with 2 on turn 269, two turns from the opponent's castle 2 (649), and lost on turn 271.
- **Feeds in clashes.** Groups of 8 walked into enemy groups on turns 251, 252 and 267. The hosted bot lost 157 soldiers in clashes.

**Cause.** `smash` prices a capture at `strikeExtra` (2) over the landing count, with no garrison to hold it. `feedStack` ignores enemy groups on the road.

### 7. Castle 4 lost by upgrading first (turns 21 to 40)

Castle 3 held 12 to 18 on turns 21 to 24 and 40 on turn 33. It bought production 3 (turn 25), production 4 (turn 33) and speed 2 (turn 37), while castle 4, a 10-soldier neutral, stood as close to it as to the opponent. The opponent sent 28 on turn 34 and took castle 4 on turn 40. The hosted bot took castle 2 for 40 on turn 42, so the middle was split, and this cost less than in the other `claw` games.

## What it did well

- Its economy matched the opponent's until turn 240.
- It struck the turn the opponent emptied castle 1, exact to the soldier (castle 4: 605 to 2).
- It then landed 941 of help in time, so the opponent's 776 died on castle 4.
- No castle of its own was ever emptied by its upgrades.

## Against the ledger's lessons

- **Never finishing, never striking in a long game: confirmed.** That covers the 180 turns frozen while ahead in soldiers, and the last 28 turns idle while losing on castles.
- **A border castle fed down beside a stocked enemy castle: confirmed, and it started every loss.** Castle 2 was fed down on turns 248 and 252, and home on turn 260.
- **Captures with few soldiers fall again: confirmed.** Castles 5 and 7.
- **Trickled attacks and long walks: confirmed.** The 10-turn strike on castle 1 was followed by 5 and 14.
- **Help in small groups dying in clashes: confirmed.**
- **The middle castles lost early: only in part.** Castle 4 was lost by upgrading first, but castle 2 was taken.
- **Saving apart while one enemy stack grows: reversed.** Here the hosted bot hoarded in one stack while the opponent kept its stock apart, and that froze both sides.
- **New:** a stack that moves after every capture empties the castle it leaves. And the server match's bot did not play the league's code.
