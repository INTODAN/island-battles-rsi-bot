# Game 503: the three main mistakes

Map star-of-david-duo; pounce played as team 2 in a server match against the opponent's hosted bot; lost on turn 140.

Each mistake is pinned to the turn where a different set of commands, on that turn alone, was clearly better. Each board was then given, alone, to the league's champion juggernaut (one turn, the same board), to see whether it makes the same mistake.

## 1. Turn 119: castles 9 and 11 held with nothing to spare (decided the game)

The enemy's stack in castle 12 split: 234 (plus 25 from castle 8) to castle 9 (238) and 251 (plus 4) to castle 11 (230), landing in 5. Castles 2 (214), 5 (218) and 10 (243) were ours. We sent 2 to castle 9 and 6 to castle 11: both held, with 5 each, and 26-soldier follow-ups took castle 11 on turn 128 and castle 9 on turn 131.

**The right move:** Hold with a margin: feed castle 9 from castles 2, 5 and 10 (speed bought where needed) so the follow-ups find hundreds, not 5.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Avoids for castle 9: the same 2 and 6 in help, then the whole rear into castle 9 (2:192, 5:192, 10:243), its stack; castle 11 is still left thin.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Repeats: the same help (2 and 6), then feeds castle 10 (2:192, 5:192, two speed upgrades there), which is not under attack, so castles 9 and 11 are left with about 5. `defend()` (`helpFor`) sends only what holds the landing itself, and the fix's `stackOf` picks castle 10 (the shortest spread) instead of a castle under attack.

## 2. Turn 126: two strikes while castle 5 faced 528

Castle 5 (220) had 253, 187 and 88 landing in 6 to 7. We sent 114 from castle 2 to castle 4 (88, production 4), 172 from castle 10 and 48 from castle 2 to castle 12 (110, with 60 landing), plus 32 to castle 5. Neither castle was taken; the soldiers were spent on the way and on their stock, and castle 5 fell on turn 132.

**The right move:** No strike: keep castles 2 and 10 stocked (castle 5 cannot be held) and help castle 9.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Avoids: `send 10->5 x32`, `up 2 speed`, `send 2->9 x6`, then feeds castle 9 (2:184, 10:239); no strike.

## 3. Turn 90: saving apart while the enemy stacks

Our five castles held 98, 102, 122, 127 and 114, each alone; the enemy fed castle 12 (264) every turn from castles 1, 3 and 7. It reached 696 by turn 117 and broke two castles at once. We sent nothing between turns 64 and 118.

**The right move:** Gather: feed one castle (castle 9 or 10) from the others and buy its speed, so the enemy's stack meets a stack.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Avoids: stack@9, feeds it from castles 2, 5, 10 and 11 and sends 92 to castle 4 (smash>4 in 6).

## The final proposed fixes on these boards

juggernaut_fix above was an early version. After all the games of this set were studied, the champion's proposed changes were extended (strikes and neutral takes priced to hold against nearby enemy castles at the speeds they can afford, a neutral paid only by castles that land together, upgrades kept back when an enemy castle could buy speed and land, a castle meeting an attacking group with half its size, no help sent to a castle it cannot save). Not yet measured in a tournament nor deployed. On the same boards it plays:

- turn 90: `["send 2->10 x98", "send 5->10 x102", "send 9->10 x122"]`
- turn 119: `["up 2 speed", "send 2->9 x2", "up 5 speed", "send 5->11 x6", "send 2->10 x192", "send 5->10 x192"]`
- turn 126: `["send 10->5 x32", "up 2 speed", "send 2->9 x6", "up 10 speed", "up 10 speed", "send 2->10 x184"]`
