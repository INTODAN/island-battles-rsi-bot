# Game 655

**Map:** `claw`. **The bot that played:** `juggernaut`, as team 1 (Blue). This was a server match imported later: the game's record says the bot ran as an uploaded package, and `turns.jsonl` is empty. **Result:** lost at the generation limit (299 turns played) on castle count, 3 castles to 4, with 3,122 soldiers to 3,661.

**The bot that played does not decide as `bots/juggernaut/main.go` does.** `uv run --locked league replay runs/real/655/demo.json --bot juggernaut --team 1` matches its commands on only 18 of 299 turns. On 282 turns the code would have sent to a neutral castle and the bot did not, starting with castle 2 on turn 8. The same code matches lobby game 486 on 66 of 66 turns. The causes below are therefore read from the bot's own logs, which are complete (one line every turn), and the code can only be compared with them.

Both sides took their two near neutrals by turn 11 (castles 3 and 5 for the hosted bot) and bought production 4 everywhere. The middle castle 4 is as far from the hosted bot's castle 3 as from the opponent's castle 6. The opponent sent 28 there on turn 34 and took it on turn 40. Castle 2, the other middle castle, kept its 10 neutral soldiers all game. From turn 44 neither side attacked again. The hosted bot fed castle 3 every other turn until it held 3,094 soldiers at speed 4, two turns from the opponent's weakest castle (castle 4, about 555) and from the neutral castle 2. It never sent a soldier anywhere but to its own castle.

## Mistakes

### 1. A stack of 3,000 that never struck, one castle behind (turns 44 to 299)

- **The log.** It was the same every turn: `turn 150: 3 castles producing 12 a turn, 1306 home; stack@3`, through to `turn 297: ... 3070 home; stack@3 feed>3 0:8 5:8`. After the opening there is no `smash>`, `take>` or `help>` in the whole game.
- **The board on turn 297.** Castle 3 held 3,070 at speed 4. The opponent's castle 4 held 551 at production 2, 3.2 units away. Neutral castle 2 held 10, 3.6 units away. Both were two-turn walks.
- **What it cost.** Taking castle 2 alone, for 11 soldiers, would have drawn the game 4 to 4. Taking castle 4 too would have won it. Either was possible on any turn from about 45 to 296.

**Cause.** By the logs, nothing in the bot ever chose a target after turn 3. By the code, it should have: `smash` would strike castle 2 for 12 on turns 60, 150 and 297, and `neutralOption` would take it from turn 8 (this is the replay's 282 disagreements). Its `guard` is 0 here, and no other enemy castle can land on castle 2 or 4 within the walk. So the code that played, or the turn it was given, differs from the league's.

One hypothesis fits most of the logged turns, but is not proven. If the uploaded package believed the game ends within a few turns (a `max_generations` of 10 to 16), `turnsLeft()` would be below every walk, and `smash` and `neutralOption` would reject every target. The bot also lacks a rule that needs no horizon at all: when its castle count is level or behind and the stack can pay for a capture, strike.

**What would have avoided it:**
- Check what the server match gives the package (`max_generations`, `turn_number`), and log the turns left.
- Never let a time horizon reject every target while the castle count loses.
- In the last turns, strike whatever can be taken when behind on castles.

### 2. The middle castle 4 given up without a fight (turns 8 to 44)

- **Castle 3 had the soldiers.** It held 20 or more from turn 24 and 40 on turn 33. It spent them on production 3 (turn 25), production 4 (turn 33) and speed 2 to 4 (turns 37 to 43).
- **The opponent took castle 4.** It paid 28 from castle 6 on turn 34 and took castle 4 with 18 on turn 40.
- **It didn't answer.** Castle 3 held 32 to 44 at speed 3 on turns 40 to 44 and struck nothing, before the opponent's 40 more landed and castle 4 reached 67 by turn 50.
- **What it decided.** That castle was the game's whole margin: 4 castles to 3, and production 14 to 12, for 250 turns.

**Cause.** As in mistake 1, no target was ever chosen. `build` meanwhile spent the stack's stock on upgrades, because nothing else claimed it.

**What would have avoided it:** take the middle neutrals while they cost 12, or strike castle 4 while it held 18 to 21, on turns 40 to 44.

### 3. Feeds that walk at speed 1, to the end (turns 25 to 299; cheap here)

Castles 0 and 5 sent 8 each to castle 3 every other turn, walking 6 and 8 turns at speed 1. That kept 40 to 56 soldiers on the road all game. The feeds of the last turns could not land before the end. `build` gives rear castles no speed. This cost little here, because the stack never moved.

## What it did well

- It had an exact, fast opening: production 2 on turn 1, and castles 3 and 5 by turn 8 with 2 to spare each.
- It reached production 4 in every castle by turn 37.
- It kept one front castle at speed 4 from turn 43.
- It never left a castle open: there was no clash and no lost castle all game.

## Against the ledger's lessons

- **The middle castles lost early: confirmed.** Castle 4 at (0, -3) decided the castle count, as the ledger's first `claw` game said the border castle does.
- **"A level game at production 4 is a draw unless someone strikes exactly": confirmed, and worse.** The hosted bot was a castle behind, with fifty times what castle 2 needed, and never struck.
- **Out-produced: confirmed, but only through the lost castle** (14 to 12). Its upgrades were not slow.
- **Saving apart while the enemy grows: contradicted in form.** Its rear fed the front every other turn. Here the hoard was the stack itself.
- **Captures that fall, trickled attacks: not seen.** It made no attack.
- **New:** the server match's bot did not play the league's code. Its lessons can't be checked in the league until that is explained.
