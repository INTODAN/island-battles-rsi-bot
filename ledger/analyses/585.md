# Game 585: the three main mistakes

Map circle (two castles 4 units apart, no neutrals); our bot was pounce_reach, playing as team 1, in a server match against the opponent's hosted bot; lost, eliminated on turn 17.

Each mistake is pinned to the turn where a different set of commands, on that turn alone, was clearly better. Each board was then given, alone, to the league's champion juggernaut (one turn, the same board), to see whether it makes the same mistake. The game has only two distinct mistakes: the third is the last chance to correct the second.

## 1. Turn 12: production 4 bought with the whole stock (decided the game)

Both castles were at production 3; castle 0 had 32, enemy castle 1 had 31, both at speed 1 (8 turns apart). We bought production 4 for 30, keeping 2. On the next turn the enemy, at 34, bought speed 2 (10) and sent 23, landing in 4 turns; castle 0 grew to 22 by then and fell by one soldier on turn 17: our only castle, and the game.

**The right move:** Buy nothing and keep the 32 home while the enemy castle, after buying speed, could land more than what we would keep plus our growth on its walk. The opponent stayed at production 3; our punisher, which never bought production 4 on this map, drew both its games at turn 300.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: up 0 production, keeping 2. Its stack's `build()` buys a level while `safeOut - cost >= guard`, and `guard()` counts only enemy castles whose walk at their *current* speed is at most `guardWalk` (4): castle 1 at speed 1 walks 8 turns, so the guard is 0, though 10 soldiers of speed bring it to 4.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Repeats: up 0 production. Its guard also counts groups landing on enemy castles, but still prices the enemy's walk at its current speed.

## 2. Turn 15: no soldiers sent to meet the 23

The enemy's 23 was 2 turns from castle 0 (14, production 4). Kept home, the castle would hold 22 at the landing: one short. We sent nothing.

**The right move:** Send 12 from castle 0 to castle 1: they meet the 23 about 0.7 units out during the next turn, before it lands. 12 is at least half of 23, so both groups stop; ours dies, the enemy's 11 stand still for 3 turns, while castle 0 (2 left, 4 a turn) grows past them.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: no commands (log `help>0 in 2`, no helper). `defend()` looks for helpers only among the other own castles (`h.id != c.id`) and never sends a castle's own soldiers to meet a group bound for it; with one castle there is no help.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Repeats, by the same rule in `defend()`.

## 3. Turn 16: the last chance, missed again

The 23 was 1 turn out; castle 0 had 18 and would hold 22 at the landing. We sent nothing; it fell with 1 enemy left in it.

**The right move:** Send 12 at castle 1: the groups meet about 0.3 units from castle 0, before the landing, and both stop; the enemy's 11 then land on a castle of 6 plus 4 a turn for 3 turns, which holds.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: no commands (log `help>0 in 1`), by the same rule in `defend()`.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Repeats, by the same rule.

## The final proposed fixes on these boards

juggernaut_fix above was an early version. After all the games were studied, the champion's proposed changes were extended and each of these boards was judged again (one turn on the same board; not yet measured in a tournament nor deployed). The proposed champion plays:

- turn 12: `[]` (avoids the mistake)
- turn 15: `["send 0->1 x12"]` (avoids the mistake)
- turn 16: `["send 0->1 x12"]` (avoids the mistake)
