# Game 500: the three main mistakes

Map star-of-david-duo; our bot batchfront, playing as team 2, in a server match against the opponent's hosted bot; lost, eliminated on turn 108.

Each mistake is pinned to the turn where a different set of commands, on that turn alone, was clearly better. Each board was then given, alone, to the league's champion juggernaut (one turn, the same board), to see whether it makes the same mistake.

## 1. Turn 70: castle 11 fed out beside a stocked enemy castle

Castle 11 (production 4) had sent 55 on turn 66 and 24 on turn 69 to castle 8; on turn 70 it held 17 and sent 16 more, keeping 5. The enemy's castle 6 held 56, 6 turns away; on the same turn it and castle 7 sent 73 at castle 11, which fell on turn 75. It was our only castle on the south side.

**The right move:** Keep castle 11's 17 and send castles 9's and 10's 13 each to it, instead of on to castle 8.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: send 9->8 13, 10->8 13 and 11->8 17, emptying castle 11 into its stack at castle 8. Rear castles feed the stack everything their own landings don't need (`feed`, with `safeOut` and no reserve).

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Avoids: send 9->11 13 and 10->11 13; rear castles keep what an enemy castle that lands before the stack's help could take.

## 2. Turn 82: a 10-turn strike at a castle the enemy refills

Castle 8 held 111 beside the enemy's castles 1 (87) and 7 (48), both 6 turns away. It sent 62 at castle 6 (12), a 10-turn walk, which castle 7 could refill in 2 to 3 turns; castle 7 and the rear sent it 81 more, it held (116 on turn 90), and the 62 were spent.

**The right move:** No strike: keep castle 8's 111 home against castle 1, and buy speed there.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats an equivalent mistake: send 8->5 111 and 9->5 9, walking the whole stack 12 turns to castle 5 and leaving castle 8 empty beside castle 1's 87. Its `stackOf` picks the own castle nearest an enemy castle, ties by id: castles 5 and 8 are both 6 turns from one, so castle 5 wins.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Avoids: still names castle 5 the stack but feeds it only 23, keeping 88 in castle 8 (the rear reserve).

## 3. Turn 85: castle 8 emptied beside castle 1's 111

Castle 8 held 73; it sent 64 more at castle 6, keeping 13, while the enemy's castle 1 held 111, 6 turns away. On turn 87 castle 1 sent 48 at castle 8 and 81 at castle 9: castle 8 fell on turn 92 and castle 9 on turn 98. The two strikes at castle 6 (126) took nothing.

**The right move:** Keep castle 8's 73, feed castle 9's soldiers into it and buy its speed.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: send 8->5 73 and 9->5 9, emptying castle 8 again (stack at castle 5 by the same id tie).

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Avoids: the stack is castle 8 (ties by distance to the other own castles); it buys speed there, sends 9->8 9 and keeps the 73.

## The final proposed fixes on these boards

juggernaut_fix above was an early version. After all the games were studied, the champion's proposed changes were extended and each of these boards was judged again (one turn on the same board; not yet measured in a tournament nor deployed). The proposed champion plays:

- turn 70: `["send 2->11 x13", "send 8->11 x11", "send 9->11 x13", "send 10->11 x13"]` (avoids the mistake)
- turn 82: `["send 10->9 x13", "send 8->5 x23", "send 9->5 x9"]` (still makes it)
- turn 85: `["send 10->11 x17", "up 5 generation_amount", "send 5->8 x13", "send 9->8 x9"]` (avoids the mistake)
