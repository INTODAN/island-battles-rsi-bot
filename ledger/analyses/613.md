# Game 613: the three main mistakes

Map archipelago; the hosted bot was batchfront_keep, playing as team 1 in a server match against the opponent's hosted bot; lost, eliminated on turn 89.

Each mistake is pinned to the turn where a different set of commands, on that turn alone, was clearly better. Each board was then given, alone, to the league's champion juggernaut (one turn, the same board), to see whether it makes the same mistake.

## 1. Turn 44: the first of 14 groups thrown at castle 3, which never fell

The enemy's castle 3 (12, production 4) sat 3 turns from castle 10 (47). We sent 10->3 38 (castle 10 kept 13); the opponent fed castle 3 with 17 and 8, and our 38 left it with 1. We then sent 13 more groups at it between turns 47 and 55 (8, 14, 20, 10, 8, 20, 8, 8, 8...), about 150 soldiers in all; castle 3 never fell, and when the opponent struck castles 10 and 6 on turns 55 and 57 every castle of ours held 5 to 13.

**The right move:** No strike at castle 3: gather in one castle at the front (0->6 9, 2->6 13, 10->6 47) and strike only with enough to beat the feeds.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Avoids: feeds its stack at castle 6 with 0->6 9, 2->6 13 and 10->6 47.

## 2. Turn 63: castle 10 emptied into castle 2, then struck with 80

Castle 10 (21, production 4) faced the enemy's castles 11, 7, 3, 1 and 5. We sent 10->2 19 and 4->2 12 to feed castle 2, leaving castle 10 with 6. On turn 64 the opponent sent 16, 17, 31, 8 and 8 at castle 10 (10); our 55 in help over the next two turns could not hold it, and it fell on turn 71 by 13. Castle 2 fell on turn 79, castle 0 on turn 84.

**The right move:** Keep castle 10's 21 home (feed castle 2 from castles 0 and 4 only); with the 55 of help it would have held by about 6.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: feeds its stack at castle 2 with 0->2 9, 4->2 13 and 10->2 21, emptying castle 10.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Repeats it too: `feedStack` makes a rear castle keep a reserve only against an enemy castle that lands on it sooner than the stack's help; the stack at castle 2 is 3 turns from castle 10 and every enemy castle 5 or more, so castle 10 keeps nothing, although five enemy castles together could land 80 on it.

## 3. Turn 53: castle 10 emptied at castle 3 beside the enemy's stocked castle 11

Castle 10 (25) sent 10->3 20 at castle 3 (35), keeping 9, while the enemy's castle 11 held 52 two to five turns away. On turn 55 castle 11 sent 60 and castle 7 17 at castle 10 (9). Castle 10 held only because castles 0, 2, 4 and 6 sent it 53, which emptied castle 6 just before the opponent's 78 landed on it; castle 6 fell on turn 62.

**The right move:** Keep castle 10's 25 home (or make it the castle the rear feeds).

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats in effect: it doesn't strike castle 3, but feeds its stack at castle 6 with 10->6 25, leaving castle 10 just as empty beside castle 11.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Avoids: its stack is castle 10 (it buys speed there and feeds it 0->10 13), so castle 10 keeps its 25.

## The final proposed fixes on these boards

juggernaut_fix above was an early version. After all the games were studied, the champion's proposed changes were extended and each of these boards was judged again (one turn on the same board; not yet measured in a tournament nor deployed). The proposed champion plays:

- turn 44: `["send 0->10 x9", "send 2->10 x13"]` (avoids the mistake)
- turn 53: `["send 0->10 x13"]` (avoids the mistake)
- turn 63: `["send 0->2 x9", "send 4->2 x13", "send 10->2 x21"]` (still makes it)
