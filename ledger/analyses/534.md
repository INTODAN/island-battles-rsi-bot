# Game 534: the three main mistakes

Map delta; the hosted bot was landgrab, playing as team 2, in a server match against the opponent's hosted bot; lost, eliminated on turn 143.

Each mistake is pinned to the turn where a different set of commands, on that turn alone, was clearly better. Each board was then given, alone, to the league's champion juggernaut (one turn, the same board), to see whether it makes the same mistake.

## 1. Turn 119: saving apart beside the enemy's stack (decided the game)

We held castles 1 (148), 3 (136), 4 (110) and 7 (168); the enemy had stacked 468 in castle 5, 2 turns from castle 7. Castle 1 was 4 turns from castle 7 at speed 3. We moved nothing; on turn 122 the enemy sent 195 from castle 5 and took castle 7 on turn 123, then took castle 4, and after a trade of retakes it took castles 3, 5 and 1 by turn 142.

**The right move:** Send castle 1's 148 into castle 7 (it lands on turn 123): castle 7 then holds over 300 beside the enemy's stack.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats, worse: its stack is castle 4 (production 2; tied with castle 7 for nearest an enemy, broken by the lower id) and it sends 1->4 148, 3->4 136 and 7->4 168, emptying castle 7 two turns from 468. The rule is `stackOf()`'s tie-break by id plus `feedStack()`, which empties every rear castle with no group bound for it.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Avoids: its tie-break by the shortest total distance makes castle 7 the stack, and it sends 1->7 148.

## 2. Turn 62: 73 soldiers thrown at castle 4, seven turns away

Castle 1 held 99; enemy castle 4 held 12, 7 turns away, beside enemy castles 6 and 0, and castle 3 (8) had 29 landing in 4. We sent 73 from castle 1 to castle 4 (and 6 to castle 3), keeping 4; castle 4 stayed the enemy's until turn 90, and on turn 65 the enemy struck castle 5 with 57.

**The right move:** Help castle 3 and keep the rest of castle 1 (or feed castle 3 or 5 as the stack).

**juggernaut on the same board:** Avoids: buys speed 3 at castle 1, sends help 1->3 6 and feeds 1->3 73 and 7->3 14.

## 3. Turn 69: castle 5 left to fall by a tie

Castle 5 held 49 (production 3); 8 + 49 landed in 2 and 20 + 12 + 11 in 5 to 6. We sent 7->5 12 and 1->5 2, but 1->4 23 at castle 4 again; castle 5 fell on turn 74, the last landing leaving it at 0.

**The right move:** Send castle 1's 23 to castle 5 too (it lands in 5).

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: `defend()` sends 7->5 12 and 1->5 2, exactly the lack its count shows with no margin, and feeds the other 23 to its stack at castle 3.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Avoids: its stack is castle 5, so it also feeds 1->5 23.

## The final proposed fixes on these boards

juggernaut_fix above was an early version. After all the games were studied, the champion's proposed changes were extended and each of these boards was judged again (one turn on the same board; not yet measured in a tournament nor deployed). The proposed champion plays:

- turn 62: `["up 1 speed", "send 1->3 x6", "send 1->3 x73"]` (avoids the mistake)
- turn 69: `["send 7->5 x12", "send 1->5 x19"]` (avoids the mistake)
- turn 119: `["send 1->7 x148", "send 3->7 x136", "send 4->7 x110"]` (avoids the mistake)
