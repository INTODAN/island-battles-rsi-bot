# Game 493: the three main mistakes

Map claw; the league bot batchfront played team 2 in a server match against the opponent's hosted bot; lost, eliminated on turn 89.

Each mistake is pinned to the turn where a different set of commands, on that turn alone, was clearly better. Each board was then given, alone, to the league's champion juggernaut (one turn, the same board), to see whether it makes the same mistake.

## 1. Turn 75: the centre castle emptied beside a stocked enemy castle (decided the game)

Castle 2 (the centre, 69, production 4) sent 52 at enemy castle 3 (12) and 16 to castle 1, keeping 5. Enemy castle 4 held 88 at speed 3, two turns away. It sent 17 on turn 76 and took castle 2 on turn 77; the 52 strike was beaten back by 31 from castles 4 and 0. Castles 7 and 1 followed, and we were out on turn 89.

**The right move:** Keep castle 2's 69 home (it grows 4 a turn and was the only castle that could face castle 4), and strike nothing.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: `send 2->1 x69`. Its `stackOf` picks the castle nearest an enemy at the slowest speed, ties by id; castles 1 and 2 tie at 6 turns, so castle 1 becomes the stack, and `feedStack` sends castle 2's whole stock there, as no enemy group is bound for castle 2 yet.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Avoids: sends nothing; its stack choice breaks the tie by the distance to the other castles and keeps castle 2 as the stack.

## 2. Turn 66: a rear castle fed to the stack beside enemy castle 4

Castle 6 (13, production 4) sent 12 to the stack at castle 2 (and 4 more on turn 67) while enemy castle 4 held 98, four turns away. On turn 69 castle 4 sent 26, which took castle 6 (13 plus growth) on turn 71. No help could land in time.

**The right move:** Keep castle 6's 13; by turn 72 it would hold over 30 and the 26 could not take it.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: `send 1->2 x9`, `send 6->2 x13`, `send 7->2 x13`. `feedStack` sends every rear castle's safe surplus while no enemy group is bound for it, whatever enemy castle stands beside it.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Repeats with the same commands. Its feed reserve counts only enemy castles that land strictly before the stack's help; castle 4 lands on castle 6 in 4 turns and castle 2's help would take 4 too, so it keeps nothing. (On turn 69, after castle 4 bought speed 3, it does keep castle 6.)

## 3. Turn 59: a strike on castle 0 that its neighbours refill

Castle 2 (106) sent 53 at enemy castle 0 (20), five turns away, with 8 and 4 from castles 6 and 7; enemy castles 3 (67) and 5 (37) are 3 turns from castle 0. Castle 3 sent 27 on turn 60 and castle 0 held with 2. The same strike was repeated on turns 63, 67 and 71 (52 to 64 each), each beaten by help from castles 3 and 5: about 240 soldiers sent, no capture, while enemy castle 4 grew to 90 unopposed.

**The right move:** No strike: keep castle 2's stock home as the guard against castle 4.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Avoids: sends nothing (its strike counts what castles 3 and 5 can land on castle 0 first).

## The final proposed fixes on these boards

juggernaut_fix above was an early version. After all the games were studied, the champion's proposed changes were extended and each of these boards was judged again (one turn on the same board; not yet measured in a tournament nor deployed). The proposed champion plays:

- turn 59: `[]` (avoids the mistake)
- turn 66: `["send 1->2 x9", "send 6->2 x13", "send 7->2 x13"]` (still makes it)
- turn 75: `["send 2->1 x69"]` (still makes it)
