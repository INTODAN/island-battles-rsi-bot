# Game 562: the three main mistakes

Map star-of-david-duo; the hosted bot was juggernaut, playing as team 2, in a server match against the opponent's hosted bot; lost (its last castle fell on turn 106, the game ended on turn 131).

Each mistake is pinned to the turn where a different set of commands, on that turn alone, was clearly better. Each board was then given, alone, to the league's champion juggernaut (one turn, the same board), to see whether it makes the same mistake.

## 1. Turn 87: a 6-turn strike from the stack beside an enemy castle of 47

Castle 5, the stack (62, speed 4), sent 46 at enemy castle 6 (8), 6 turns away, keeping 16, while enemy castle 11 (47) stood 5.8 from it. The strike took nothing; our castle 9 (4) fell on turn 90, and castle 5 later fell on turn 100.

**The right move:** No strike: keep the 62 in castle 5, which holds castle 11's 47 in place.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: smash>6 with 46 and 10->5 8. `smash` prices only enemy castles that land before the strike, and the stack's `guard` only those within `guardWalk` (4 turns); castle 11 is 6 turns away.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Repeats an equivalent: no strike, but it names castle 10 the stack and feeds it all 62 of castle 5 (feed>10 5:62), leaving castle 5 empty beside the 47; its rear reserve counts only enemy castles that land before the stack's help, and castle 11 and castle 10 are both 6 turns from castle 5.

## 2. Turn 96: 81 soldiers sent on a 35-turn walk to a falling castle

Castle 0 (40), taken on turn 92, had about 110 enemy soldiers landing on it within 3 turns. Castle 2 (81, speed 1) sent all 81 to it, 17.3 away, a 35-turn walk, and castle 5 sent its 18. Castle 0 fell on turn 98; castle 5, left with 0 beside enemy castle 11 (28), fell on turn 100 to 47; castle 2 on turn 106.

**The right move:** Let castle 0 go: keep the 81 in castle 2 and the 18 in castle 5 (or move castle 2's soldiers to castle 5).

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: feed>0 2:81 5:18. `stackOf` names castle 0, the castle nearest the enemy, although its landings take it in 3 turns, and `feedStack` sends every rear castle's whole stock there at any distance.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Avoids: it skips the falling castle, names castle 5 the stack and sends only 2->5 32.

## 3. Turn 65: castle 8 taken among four enemy castles

The stack, castle 9 (32), sent 26 at enemy castle 8 (12), 3 turns away; enemy castles 1, 4, 7 and the centre (taken by the enemy the same turn) were 5 to 6 turns from it. Castle 8 fell to us on turn 67 with 2; we fed it about 100 soldiers over 16 turns while castle 11 (4, feeding it too) fell on turn 74, and the enemy took castle 8 back on turn 83.

**The right move:** No strike: keep the stack in castle 9 and stop castle 11 from feeding away its last soldiers.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: smash>8 with 26 and 10->9 8. `smash` counts only enemy castles that land before the strike.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Repeats: its retake price counts enemy castles only within `guardWalk` (4 turns), and on this map they are 5 to 6 turns away.

## The final proposed fixes on these boards

juggernaut_fix above was an early version. After all the games of this set were studied, the champion's proposed changes were extended (strikes and neutral takes priced to hold against nearby enemy castles at the speeds they can afford, a neutral paid only by castles that land together, upgrades kept back when an enemy castle could buy speed and land, a castle meeting an attacking group with half its size, no help sent to a castle it cannot save). Not yet measured in a tournament nor deployed. On the same boards it plays:

- turn 65: `["send 9->8 x26", "send 10->9 x8"]`
- turn 87: `["send 5->10 x62"]`
- turn 96: `["send 2->5 x32"]`
