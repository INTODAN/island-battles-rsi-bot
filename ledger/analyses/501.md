# Game 501: the three main mistakes

Map star-of-david-duo; the hosted bot was speedhelp_fullfeed, playing as team 1, in a server match against the opponent's hosted bot; lost on turn 120.

Each mistake is pinned to the turn where a different set of commands, on that turn alone, was clearly better. Each board was then given, alone, to the league's champion juggernaut (one turn, the same board), to see whether it makes the same mistake.

## 1. Turn 94: castle 7 left to fall (decided the game)

Castle 7 held 72 (production 4); the enemy sent 95 from castle 8, landing in 3, so castle 7 would hold about 84 and fall by about 11. Castle 1 held 124 at speed 3, 4 turns away. We sent nothing on turns 93 to 95; castle 7 fell on turn 96, and with it the centre: castle 0 fell on turn 100, castle 3 on turn 108 and castle 1 (243) on turn 119, each to a group no castle could still reach in time.

**The right move:** Buy speed 4 at castle 1 (its group then lands in 3) and send castle 1's 12 or more to castle 7.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Avoids: `defend()` buys speed 4 at castle 1 and sends 1->7 12, landing with the enemy group. (It also sends 1->0 82 and 3->0 60 to feed its stack at castle 0.)

## 2. Turn 66: castle 8 fed the stack beside a stocked enemy castle

Castle 8 held 16 (production 4, speed 2); enemy castle 9 held 27 at speed 3, 4 turns away. We sent 10 from castle 8 to the stack at castle 7, leaving 10; on turn 67 the enemy sent 31 from castle 9, castle 8 held about 26 at the landing and fell on turn 70. Our stack at castle 7 was 6 turns away and could not help.

**The right move:** Keep castle 8's soldiers home (feed the stack from castles 1 and 3 only): with them castle 8 holds about 36 against 31.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats, worse: its stack is castle 0 (production 1, on the map's edge, nearest an enemy by walk with ties to the lowest id), and it sends 8->0 16, 6->0 16, 7->0 32 and 1->0 8, emptying all three front castles. The rule is `feedStack()`: every rear castle with no enemy group bound for it sends all its `safeOut`.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Repeats: its stack is castle 7, it buys speed 3 there and sends 8->7 16, 6->7 16 and 1->7 8. Its reserve rule keeps soldiers only against enemy castles that land strictly before the stack's help; with the speed bought that turn the help also takes 4 turns, so castle 8 keeps nothing.

## 3. Turn 70: castle 6 fed the stack beside enemy castle 11

Castle 6 held 32; enemy castle 11 held 36 at speed 3, 4 turns away. We sent 18 from castle 6 to castle 7; on turn 71 the enemy sent 40 from castle 11 and castle 6 fell on turn 74 with 30 in it. Kept home, castle 6 would have held about 50.

**The right move:** Keep castle 6's 32 home; feed the stack from castles 1 and 3.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: sends 6->0 32 (and 7->0 24, 1->0 12, 3->0 12) to its stack at castle 0, by the same `feedStack()` rule.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Repeats: sends 6->7 32. Castle 11 lands in 4 and castle 7's help (speed 3) also takes 4, so the reserve rule (`walk < back`) keeps nothing.

## The final proposed fixes on these boards

juggernaut_fix above was an early version. After all the games of this set were studied, the champion's proposed changes were extended (strikes and neutral takes priced to hold against nearby enemy castles at the speeds they can afford, a neutral paid only by castles that land together, upgrades kept back when an enemy castle could buy speed and land, a castle meeting an attacking group with half its size, no help sent to a castle it cannot save). Not yet measured in a tournament nor deployed. On the same boards it plays:

- turn 66: `["up 7 speed", "send 1->7 x8", "send 6->7 x16", "send 8->7 x16"]`
- turn 70: `["send 8->9 x16", "up 0 generation_amount", "send 1->7 x12", "send 3->7 x12", "send 6->7 x32"]`
- turn 94: `["up 1 speed", "send 1->7 x12", "send 1->7 x82", "send 3->7 x60"]`
