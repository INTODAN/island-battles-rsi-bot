# Game 494: the three main mistakes

Map claw; the hosted bot was landgrab, playing as team 2 in a server match against the opponent's hosted bot; lost, eliminated on turn 94.

Each mistake is pinned to the turn where a different set of commands, on that turn alone, was clearly better. Each board was then given, alone, to the league's champion juggernaut (one turn, the same board), to see whether it makes the same mistake.

## 1. Turn 56: 42 soldiers sunk into castle 2, a production-1 castle

Castle 2 (the enemy's, 9, production 1) sat in the middle, 5 turns from castle 1. We sent 1->2 18 (castle 1 kept 2 after its growth) and, on turn 58, 6->2 18 and 7->2 6. Castle 2 fell to us on turn 65 with 16 and was lost again on turn 70 to 16 from castle 0. Meanwhile the opponent's production went from 11 to 14 levels and ours stayed at 6 to 8.

**The right move:** No strike at castle 2: buy production at castle 7 (7 had 20) and keep castle 1's 18 home.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Avoids: buys production 3 at castle 7 and sends nothing; on turn 58 it also sends nothing.

## 2. Turn 72: castle 1 emptied at castle 2 while castle 6 fell

An enemy group of 21 from castle 4 landed on castle 6 (16, production 2) in 2 turns; no help could land in time. We sent 1->2 18 at castle 2 (5, 5 turns away), leaving castle 1 with 3, and on turn 74 7->2 14. The 18 only wore castle 2 down to 1, the 14 met castle 4's 16 there, and on turn 78 the opponent struck castle 1 (15) with 56. Castle 1 survived that with 1 and fell on turn 88; castle 7 on turn 93.

**The right move:** Keep castle 1's 18 home (and castle 7's 14 on turn 74, or feed it to castle 1): castle 1 is the last stocked castle and the next target.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Avoids: sends nothing on turn 72 (help>6 can't land in time) and on turn 74 feeds castle 1 (7->1 14).

## 3. Turn 29: a neutral taken with 2, which handed castle 4 to the opponent

Castle 4 (neutral, 10) is exactly as far from castle 1 as from the enemy's castle 0 (12 turns at speed 1). We sent 1->4 10 and 6->4 2 (castle 1 kept 2). They took castle 4 on turn 40 with 2; the opponent's 9 from castle 0, sent one turn later, landed on turn 41 and took it with 6. Alone, the 9 could not have taken the neutral 10. Castle 4 became the opponent's forward castle, from which it later took castle 6 and struck castle 1.

**The right move:** No send: keep the 10 at castle 1 for its production upgrade, and leave castle 4 to whoever pays the neutral's 10 first.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: exactly the same take>4 (6->4 2, 1->4 10).

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Repeats it too: `neutralOption` takes a neutral that no enemy castle reaches strictly sooner (a tie counts as ours), at its count plus `neutralExtra` (2), and the hold it adds for castle 0 is castle 0's spare less the capture's growth over castle 0's whole 12-turn walk, which is 0.
