# Game 564: the three main mistakes

Map archipelago; the hosted bot was juggernaut, playing as team 1, in a server match against the opponent's hosted bot; lost (its last castle fell on turn 91).

Each mistake is pinned to the turn where a different set of commands, on that turn alone, was clearly better. Each board was then given, alone, to the league's champion juggernaut (one turn, the same board), to see whether it makes the same mistake.

## 1. Turn 73: the stack of 155 moved out of castle 0 (decided the game)

Castle 0 held 155 (speed 4) beside enemy castle 10 (168, speed 4). It sent all 155 to our castle 2 (8) next door, keeping 0 (4 the next turn). The enemy took castle 0 on turn 75 with 25; castle 2, our last castle, sat on 165 to 171 for 16 turns and fell on turn 91 to a single strike of 176.

**The right move:** Send nothing: castle 0 with 155 and production 4 held castle 10 off, and castle 2 (8) beside it was the cheaper castle to lose.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: feed>2 0:155. `stackOf` picks the castle nearest an enemy castle (castle 2, tied, by id), and `feedStack` sends the old stack's whole `safeOut` to it as a rear castle.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Avoids: also names castle 2 the stack but sends nothing; castle 10 lands on castle 0 before the stack's help could, so castle 0 keeps its reserve.

## 2. Turn 47: the stack spent on the neutral centre castle

Castle 6, the stack (39), sent 32 at neutral castle 8 (30, production 1), 3 turns away, keeping 7, with enemy castle 11 (42) 2.1 from castle 8. It took castle 8 on turn 49 with 2. The same turn the enemy sent 85 at our castle 10 (4), which fell on turn 52; castle 8, fed about 70 more, fell on turn 56.

**The right move:** No strike: keep the 39 in castle 6 (and send castle 10 help when the 85 showed).

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: smash>8 with 32 and feeds 0->6 8, 2->6 8. A neutral target is priced at its soldiers plus `strikeExtra` and only checked for enemy castles that reach it sooner; castle 11's 42, beside it, isn't counted.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Repeats an equivalent: no strike, but its new `stackOf` tie-break (shortest total distance to the other own castles) names castle 10 the stack and feeds it 6:16, 0:8, 2:8, about 36 into the castle the enemy struck with 85 that turn.

## 3. Turn 70: castle 6 fed the stack beside an enemy castle of 100

Castle 6 (8) sent 8 to castle 0, keeping 4 (0 after the send), with enemy castle 10 (100, speed 4) 3.4 away. The enemy sent 16 at it the next turn and took it on turn 72.

**The right move:** Keep the 8 home: castle 6 would have held about 16 when the 16 landed, and the opponent priced its strike on the castle it saw emptied.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: feed>0 6:8. `feedStack` gives a rear castle's whole `safeOut` when no enemy group is yet bound for it.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Avoids: sends nothing (castle 10 lands on castle 6 before the stack's help, so castle 6 keeps its reserve).

## The final proposed fixes on these boards

juggernaut_fix above was an early version. After all the games of this set were studied, the champion's proposed changes were extended (strikes and neutral takes priced to hold against nearby enemy castles at the speeds they can afford, a neutral paid only by castles that land together, upgrades kept back when an enemy castle could buy speed and land, a castle meeting an attacking group with half its size, no help sent to a castle it cannot save).
