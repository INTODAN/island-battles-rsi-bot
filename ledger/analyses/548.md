# Game 548: the three main mistakes

Map estuary; the hosted bot was speedhelp_fullfeed, playing as team 2, in a server match against the opponent's hosted bot; lost at the turn limit (turn 300), 3 castles to 5, the game going by castle count.

Each mistake is pinned to the turn where a different set of commands, on that turn alone, was clearly better. Each board was then given, alone, to the league's champion juggernaut (one turn, the same board), to see whether it makes the same mistake.

## 1. Turn 295: the last stack sent where it could not land in time (decided the game)

Five turns before the end we held castles 1, 3, 4 (6), 5 (828) and, landing in 2, a strike that took castle 6. Enemy castle 0 held 926, 3 turns from castles 4 and 5. Castle 5 sent 828 to castle 2, 6 turns away (it would land after turn 300), keeping 4. Castle 0 then sent 12 at castle 4 and 63 at castle 5; castle 4 fell on turn 298 and castle 6 on turn 300, and we ended with 3 castles to 5.

**The right move:** Keep castle 5's 828 home and send 20 or so to castle 4 (2 turns), so both hold to the end; we then end with 4 or 5 castles.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats in part: its stack becomes castle 4 and it sends 1->4 124, 3->4 828 and 5->4 828. Castle 4 holds, but castle 5 is left with 0, three turns from 926. The rule is `feedStack()`, which empties every rear castle with no enemy group bound for it, even in the last turns and beside a stocked enemy castle.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Repeats the same sends: its reserve keeps soldiers only against enemy castles that land before the stack's help, and castle 4 is 2 turns from castle 5 while castle 0 is 3.

## 2. Turn 292: castle 4 taken with a margin that couldn't hold

Neutral castle 4 (25, production 1) stood 2 turns from castle 5 (846) and 3 from enemy castle 0 (914). We sent 30, took it on turn 293 with 5, and it was lost on turn 298 to a group of 12.

**The right move:** Take castle 4 with 100 or more from castle 5's 846, or take it on turn 296 so that nothing can land on it before the end.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Repeats: `smash()` sends 5->4 27 (the neutral's 25 plus 2), then feeds castle 5. For a neutral target it counts only the soldiers there, not the enemy castles that can retake it.

**juggernaut_fix (proposed, not yet measured nor deployed):** Repeats the same 27: its hold pricing is applied only to targets the enemy owns, not to neutrals.

## 3. Turn 77: a retake of castle 7 that couldn't hold

Castle 7 (production 1) had been taken on turn 74 with 5 and lost on turn 76. On turn 77 castle 5 (54) sent 26 at it (20 there); we took it on turn 80 with 2 and lost it on turn 84 to enemy castle 2's groups. From turn 85 to turn 298, 23 groups of 5 to 10 (249 soldiers) were sent into castle 7, which grew to over 1000.

**The right move:** Keep castle 5's 54 home and grow it; strike castle 7 only with enough to hold against castle 2.

**juggernaut on the same board:** Avoids the retake: it sends no strike at castle 7. It does make mistake 2's thin neutral capture instead (5->4 27, with castle 0's 54 three turns away), and buys speed and feeds castle 5.

## The final proposed fixes on these boards

juggernaut_fix above was an early version. After all the games of this set were studied, the champion's proposed changes were extended (strikes and neutral takes priced to hold against nearby enemy castles at the speeds they can afford, a neutral paid only by castles that land together, upgrades kept back when an enemy castle could buy speed and land, a castle meeting an attacking group with half its size, no help sent to a castle it cannot save). Not yet measured in a tournament nor deployed. On the same boards it plays:

- turn 77: `["up 5 speed", "send 1->5 x20", "send 3->5 x16"]`
- turn 292: `["send 1->5 x830", "send 3->5 x816"]`
- turn 295: `["send 5->4 x828"]`
