# Game 657

**Map:** `claw`. **The bot that played:** `juggernaut`, as team 2 (Red). `uv run --locked league replay runs/real/657/demo.json --bot juggernaut --team 2` matches its commands on 299 of 299 turns, so the causes below are read from `bots/juggernaut/main.go` as well as from its logs. **Result:** lost at the generation limit (299 turns played) on castle count, 3 castles to 5, with 2,211 soldiers to 3,008. The server took all 297 of its answers, and no turn was passed (slowest 268 ms).

The six neutral castles were split by turn 11, three each. Then the hosted bot sent two split takes at the two contested neutral castles in the middle, castle 2 on turn 8 and castle 4 on turn 12. Each landed in pieces, captured its castle with 2 or 3 soldiers, and lost it to a small group of the opponent's that landed right after (castle 2 on turn 17, castle 4 on turn 24). From then on it had 3 castles to 5, and its production stayed at 12 a turn against 19. It fed everything into one stack at castle 6, which held 882 soldiers by turn 124. On turn 124 the stack took castle 2 with an exact strike of 255, which made it 4 castles each from turn 125. Then the game froze for 170 turns. The new stack at castle 2 grew to 3,179 and never struck, and its three rear castles held 4 to 12 soldiers each. On turn 297 it finally struck castle 5 with 1,006. But on the same turn the opponent sent 16 and 28 at two of its 4-soldier rear castles, and took castle 6 on turn 298 and castle 1 on turn 299. A 4-to-4 draw ended as a 3-to-5 loss.

## Mistakes

### 1. Two contested neutral castles taken in pieces with a 2-soldier margin, and lost the turn after (turns 8 to 24)

- **Turn 8, castle 2** (the centre, neutral with 10): `take>2 6:3 1:9`. It sent 3 from castle 6 (3.6 away) and 9 from castle 1 (5.0 away). The 3 landed first, on turn 15, and took castle 2 down to 7. On turn 10 the opponent sent 5 from castle 3 (3.6 away, the same distance as castle 6). On turn 17 the 9 landed and took castle 2 with 2 soldiers. The opponent's 5 landed in the same turn and took it back with 3.
- **Turn 12, castle 4** (neutral with 10): `take>4 6:4 1:9`. It sent 4 from castle 6 (3.2 away) and 9 from castle 1 (5.8 away, a 12-turn walk). The 4 landed on turn 19 and took castle 4 down to 6. On turn 13 the opponent sent 10 from castle 0. That group would not have taken a full neutral of 10, but now it was enough. The 9 took castle 4 on turn 23 with 3 soldiers. The opponent's 10 landed on turn 24 and took it with 6. On turn 24 the bot's log reads `help>4 in 1` with no sender: no castle could land in one turn.
- **Cost:** 25 soldiers spent for nothing, and two castles that went to the opponent instead. From turn 24 it was 3 castles to 5. It never got back to 5: production was 12 a turn against 19 from turn 45 to turn 125, and 15 against 16 after.
- **Cause:** `neutralOption` (`main.go`) treats a neutral castle as its own when `b.sooner(b.enemies, target) < b.sooner(b.own, target)` is false. Both castles were at exactly equal walks from its castle 6 and the opponent's castle 3 (8 turns to castle 2, 7 to castle 4), so the tie counted them as its own side. The capture's hold is priced by `enemySpare`, which assumes the opponent keeps the main line's reserve against each of the bot's castles. The opponent kept none, and sent 5 and 10 soldiers that the price had not counted. The price also uses the walk of the farthest payer, so the nearer group landed 2 to 4 turns early. It softened the neutral castle and showed the opponent exactly how much it needed. Once the groups were walking, nothing topped up a capture that an enemy group would retake. `defend` only looks at castles the bot already owns, and `neutralOption` skips a target that any group is bound for.
- **What would have avoided it:** count an equal walk as contested (`<=`), and leave such a castle to be struck after the enemy takes it thin, as the code's own pounce comment says of contested neutral castles. Or price the hold by the whole stock of the enemy castles that land soon after the capture, as `guard` does for the stack. Payers whose walks differ by several turns should be sent so that they land together.

### 2. A stack of up to 3,179 that neither struck nor upgraded for 170 turns (turns 126 to 296)

- **What happened:** after taking castle 2 on turn 125, the stack moved there. That castle had been the opponent's, with production 3 and speed 2. It stayed at production 3 and speed 2 until turn 296, while it grew from 659 to 3,179. Its only strike before the end was on turn 297. From turn 241 to the end, the opponent's castle 0 held 4 to 12 soldiers. On turn 240 it had sent all 486 of its soldiers to castle 4, and after that it fed castle 4 every three turns (`left with 4` on turns 243 to 294). Taking it in those 56 turns would have meant 5 castles to 3.
- **Cause:** `build` buys the stack's next level only while `safeOut - cost >= guard`. `guard` adds up the whole stock of every enemy castle within `guardWalk` (4) turns, which was castles 3, 4 and 5, about 2,400 to 3,900 soldiers. That was always more than the stack held. So the stack kept speed 2, and even a 10-soldier production level was refused. Then in `smash`, at speed 2 every target was 4 or 5 turns away. The price adds the whole stock of every other enemy castle with `1 + travel <= walk`. For castle 0 (5 turns away), castles 3, 4 and 5 all qualify, so the price was about all of the opponent's soldiers, and no target was ever affordable. Only on turn 296, when `walk < b.turnsLeft()` dropped castles 3 and 5 from `guard`, did it buy production 4 and speed 4, and strike the next turn.
- **What would have avoided it:** buy the stack's speed whatever the guard says, once the castle holds more than any one enemy castle can land. And price a strike by what the enemy can actually add in time, not the sum of every castle's stock. The ledger has said since iteration 5 that this sum makes a bot too timid. Castle 0 held 4 to 12 soldiers, five units from the stack, for the last 56 turns. It was a castle that the count needed.

### 3. Rear castles at 4 soldiers through the finish, taken on the last two turns (turns 297 to 299)

- **What happened:** castles 1, 6 and 7 fed the stack 8 soldiers every other turn, from turn 126 to the end, and held 4 to 12 each (`feed>2 1:8 6:8 7:8`). On turn 297 the opponent's castle 4 (1,934 soldiers, speed 4 bought that turn) sent 16 at castle 6 and 28 at castle 1. Both castles held 4. Castle 6 fell on turn 298, with `help>6 in 1` and no helper. Castle 1 fell on turn 299, with `help>1 in 2` and no helper. The stack at castle 2 is 3.6 and 5.0 away, which is two and three turns even at speed 4. On turn 299 the bot sent 2,129 from castle 2 to castle 1 (`feed>1 2:2129`), a group that could not land before the game ended. The capture of castle 5 made it 5 castles to 4 for one turn, then 3 to 5.
- **Cause:** `feedStack` sends a rear castle's whole `safeOut`, which keeps nothing beyond its own landings, once it has `feedLeast` (8). The top comment states the rule: "no reserve in the rear. The stack is the reserve." Nothing changes in the last `finishTurns`, when every castle counts the same and a rear castle of 4 is the cheapest one on the board. `feedStack` also feeds on the last turn, when no group can land.
- **What would have avoided it:** in the finish, stop feeding, so that each rear castle keeps its growth: 4 a turn for 30 turns is about 120, and an attack of 16 or 28 would then fail. Better, send part of the stack back to the castles the enemy's stacks can reach within a turn or two before striking, as game 656's analysis also concluded. Send no group that lands after the game ends.

### 4. Emptying the old stack next to a 409-soldier enemy castle (turn 126)

- **What happened:** the turn after castle 2 was taken with 2 soldiers, castle 6 sent all 651 of its soldiers there (`feed>2 1:8 6:651 7:8`) and kept 4. The opponent's castle 4 held 409, 3.2 units away. Castle 2 held 2 and then 5 soldiers for two turns, beside the opponent's castles 3 (310), 4 (409) and 5 (308). The opponent didn't strike, so this cost nothing here.
- **Cause:** `stackOf` names the castle nearest the enemy as the stack, and `feedStack` moves everything there at once. The castle that gives up the stack becomes a rear castle with no reserve.
- **What would have avoided it:** a castle that stops being the stack should keep what the enemy castles within `guardWalk` could land on it. That is the stack's own guard rule, applied to the castle that was the stack.

## What it did well

- **Its strikes were exact and they held.** Castle 2 on turn 124 cost 255 against 250 (`smash>2 in 2 need 255`). Castle 5 on turn 297 cost 1,006 against 996.
- **It defended in time.** On turn 48 the opponent sent 48 soldiers at castle 7, and the next turn the stack sent 21 there (`help>7 in 5 6:21`). Castle 7 held.
- **Its opening was quick.** It bought production 2 on turn 1 and took castles 6 and 7 by turn 8, before the opponent had a second castle.
- **It never failed a turn.** All 297 answers were accepted.

## Against the ledger's lessons

- **Confirms "Counting everything an enemy castle's owner could send to it makes a bot too timid"** (iteration 5), and game 656's finding. `smash` and `guard` both sum the opponent's whole stock. Here that kept a 3,000-soldier stack still and at speed 2 for 170 turns, beside an enemy castle that held 4 to 12 for the last 56 turns.
- **Confirms "A bot with no reserve loses its castles to strikes sized to what it left"** (iteration 40) and game 656's "fed its whole stack forward". The groups of 16 and 28 that decided the game were sized to the 4 soldiers each castle held. The same thin rear was there for 170 turns before that, and on turn 126 beside a 409-soldier enemy castle.
- **Confirms "A castle taken with 1 soldier near the enemy falls unless another castle reinforces it"** and "A castle taken exactly must also outlast the next enemy castle along" (iterations 1 and 13). Castles 2 and 4 were taken with 2 and 3 soldiers, and each fell to the next enemy group, which was 5 and 10 soldiers.
- **Confirms the trickled attack and the long walk.** Each take arrived in two parts, 2 to 4 turns apart, and one part walked 12 turns. The early part softened the neutral castle for the opponent.
- **Confirms "A frozen game is lost by whoever breaks it, unless it was lost anyway"**, from the other side. The opponent broke the freeze only on turn 297, when the hosted bot couldn't answer. The hosted bot's late strike was answered at once on its thin castles.
- **Contradicts nothing.** The lesson "a castle with nothing left to buy should feed the front" (game 474) is taken here to its end: feeding everything left no castle that could hold.
